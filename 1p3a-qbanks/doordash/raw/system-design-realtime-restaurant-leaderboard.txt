System Design: Real-Time Restaurant Leaderboard
Design a system that continuously displays the ten restaurants with the most orders in a sliding one-hour window. Second-level freshness is acceptable, approximate answers are allowed, and extensions cover per-city leaderboards, late or duplicate events, and stream-processor recovery.

Requirements

Functional

Ingest order events continuously.

Display the ten restaurants with the most orders during the previous sliding hour.

Update the displayed ranking with roughly second-level freshness.

Support an optional extension for historical lookback.

Follow-up: return a separate top ten for each user's city.

Non-functional

Approximate results are acceptable if the error trade-off is explained.

Handle restaurants whose event volume is much hotter than the rest of the keyspace.

Handle late and duplicate order events using event time and a unique event ID.

Recover the streaming computation after a worker failure without losing the ability to replay retained events.

Design decisions

Choose a representation for the one-hour sliding window and explain its update cost.

Choose between exact aggregation and a memory-efficient approximate counter, including the direction of estimation error.

Choose a partitioning strategy that avoids concentrating a hot restaurant on one worker.

Notes

Clarify order-event rate, acceptable freshness, exact versus approximate output, sliding versus fixed windows, and historical-query requirements before drawing the pipeline.

Minute buckets make expiration cheaper but can retain events for up to one extra bucket width; second-level display refresh does not by itself make window membership second-accurate. Use finer buckets or an event-time expiry queue when that error is unacceptable.

A Count-Min Sketch overestimates point counts because of collisions and cannot enumerate the top restaurants by itself. Pair it with a bounded heavy-hitter candidate structure, then rank candidates by their estimates; otherwise retain exact per-key counts.

The per-city extension changes the aggregation key to include both city and restaurant.

Preparation

Draw the full order-event pipeline and implement the sliding hour with minute buckets, including bucket expiry.

Drill hot-key splitting and the second aggregation step required to merge a restaurant's partial counts.

Compare exact counters with a Count-Min Sketch and explain why its estimate is biased upward.

Rehearse duplicate suppression, late-event policy, checkpoint recovery, and replay from the message queue.
