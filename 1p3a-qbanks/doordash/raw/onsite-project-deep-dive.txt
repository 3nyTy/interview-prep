Requirements

Pick one project from your background. The recruiter typically sends a prep template before the round listing the expected dimensions:

Problem statement — what you were solving, why it mattered.

Your role — IC, lead, contributor; scope and ownership.

Methods / approach — what you tried, what you picked, why.

Difficulties — what was hard, how you handled ambiguity.

Success metrics — offline (model metrics), online (A/B), business (revenue, retention).

The round is 60 minutes; the interviewer typically gives 2–3 minutes of intro and then drills for 50+ minutes on tradeoffs.

Notes

DoorDash's prep template is detailed and unusual — use it. Multiple candidates report that following the template literally (problem → role → methods → difficulties → metrics, in that order) made the round flow smoothly.

The interviewer especially probes:

Trade-offs: why this design and not that one; what you'd change today.

Metrics: offline metric definition (precision/recall/AUC/RMSE), how it relates to the online metric, how the online metric relates to business outcomes (orders, GMV, conversion).

Post-launch behavior: what surprised you after launch, what you measured, what you iterated.

For Staff / EM levels, expect XFN-impact drilling: who you worked with, how decisions were made, how you handled disagreement.

Common failure mode: walking through the project as a narrative instead of answering each follow-up directly. Be concise; let the interviewer drive.

Slide deck is optional but recommended for Staff and EM rounds; a 1-page architecture diagram materially helps.

Don't pick the most recent project if it's still in flight — pick one with shipped metrics and a clear retrospective.

The slide advice extends below Staff: in screening-slot deep dives the interviewer often follows the candidate's deck as the round's structure, interrupting for details. Expect one make-or-break ownership question — in a system this large, what exactly did you own — and answer with your specific scope rather than the team's.

Embedded system-design format

The project walkthrough can occupy the first approximately 25 minutes of a 75-minute system-design round instead of appearing as a standalone hour. Prepare a compact architecture-first version of the deck that can survive interruptions and still leave enough time for the assigned design prompt. MLE screening versions likewise follow recruiter-provided preparation material and use the candidate's slide deck as the conversation backbone.

Preparation

Use the DoorDash prep template literally. Fill it in before the round; rehearse to a friend in under 10 minutes.

For each section, prepare 2–3 follow-up branches the interviewer might dive into (deeper into architecture, deeper into metrics, deeper into team dynamics).

For Staff / EM: prepare a 1-slide architecture diagram and a 1-slide metrics dashboard.

Prepare a clear "what I'd do differently" answer — interviewers almost always ask, and a generic "more testing" answer underperforms.
