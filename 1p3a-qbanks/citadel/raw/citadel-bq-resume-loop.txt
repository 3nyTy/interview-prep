Behavioral / Resume Drilling Across the Loop
Citadel and Citsec both front-load resume / behavioral content into nearly every round. Phone screens dedicate 10-30 minutes to resume drilling before coding; onsite has dedicated team-fit / culture rounds; the loop typically closes with a final leadership call on the Citadel side.

Requirements

Expected behavioral coverage across the loop:

Phone screens: 10-30 minutes of resume / project drilling at the top of every technical screen. Common openers: "why Citadel," "what's the difference between Citadel and Citadel Securities," "walk me through your most impactful project."

Onsite BQ round (especially NXT and Citadel hedge-fund loops): 1 dedicated hour. Standard prompts — handle conflict, time you disagreed with a manager, scope-clarification when requirements were ambiguous.

Team match interviews (Citadel hedge fund only): each match round opens with "tell me about yourself" and runs primarily as a culture / fit conversation. Multiple match rounds across different teams stack on top of one another.

Final leadership call (Citsec and Citadel both): 30-45 minutes with a senior engineer / PM after technical clearance. Less drilling, more candidate-driven Q&A about the team and decision-making process.

Project-impact probe: explain the most technically challenging work in your background and whether a feature directly helped commercialize a product. These may arrive as one combined prompt, so be ready with either one integrated story or two clearly separated examples.

Recent challenge probes: explain the hardest problem you solved recently and walk through a concrete challenge, the decisions you made, and how you resolved it. These prompts can precede either coding or a design discussion, so keep the story technically specific and concise.

Notes

The "why Citadel vs Citsec" question is the single most common opener. Have a 60-second answer that demonstrates you understand the two entities are operationally distinct (see citadel-vs-citsec briefing for the substance).

Resume drilling at Citadel tends to be deeper than at typical FAANG loops: interviewers chase down specific metrics, design trade-offs, and failure modes 3-4 follow-ups deep. Prepare each resume line item with one paragraph of additional detail you can volunteer.

For research / QR / EQR candidates, the deep-dive is structured like an alpha-research walk-through (hypothesis → data → factor construction → diagnostics → results). See the eqr-alpha-factor-deep-dive entry for the canonical sub-prompts.

Team-match rounds reward warmth and clarity about what you want next, not technical breadth. Candidates report that being explicit about preferred role (engineering vs research split, asset class, location) shortens the match phase materially.

Behavioral failure modes most often cited in this loop: vague metrics ("made it faster" without numbers), unclear personal contribution on team projects, inability to articulate what you would have done differently.

For experienced / lateral SWE candidates, early screens can be entirely behavioral with no coding at all — back-to-back conversational rounds that drill resume and projects. A frequent opener is "walk me through the hardest engineering project from your recent experience," followed by depth probes on the toughest technical decision in it.

Interviewer engagement varies sharply. Some candidates describe cold, low-affect screens that wrap up well under the scheduled time (a 45-minute round ending in ~20 minutes) — read this as interviewer style rather than a verdict, and keep your answers tight and well-structured regardless of the signals you're getting back.

In Citsec phone screens the resume half can consume a full 30 minutes of the hour, with probing concentrated on trading-relevant project work — expect detailed follow-ups on the technical bottlenecks you hit and how you optimized them before the round pivots to the technical exercise.

Project discussions can drill into what you built and why each design choice was made before coding begins; a 15-minute resume segment can still be the most demanding part of an internship phone screen.

In the September 2026 SWE internship format, two 45-minute virtual interviews each include self-introduction and resume discussion before coding. A separate hedge-fund first-round variant uses only a brief introduction and then spends the session on coding and design, leaving no time for behavioral questions.

Preparation

Write 6-8 STAR-format stories covering conflict, ambiguity, ownership, mentorship, technical leadership, and a project you would do differently with hindsight. Re-use across rounds.

Pre-write a 60-second "why Citadel / why Citsec" answer that demonstrates structural understanding of the firm. Avoid generic "I want to work at a top quant fund" framing.

For research candidates, prepare the alpha-research deep-dive talk track (hypothesis → data → factor → diagnostics → live results). Practice landing it in 5-7 minutes; the interviewer will interrupt with follow-ups, so build natural pause points.

For Citadel (hedge fund) candidates, identify 2-3 target teams or asset classes in advance and articulate why each fits your background. This focuses team-match conversations and avoids the open-ended drift that prolongs the match phase.
