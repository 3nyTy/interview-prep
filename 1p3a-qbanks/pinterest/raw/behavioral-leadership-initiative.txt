Requirements

45–60 minutes of behavioral questions, usually with the hiring manager or a senior IC. Recurring prompts:

Tell me about a failure or mistake and what you changed afterward.

Tell me about a project you led end-to-end. Where did the requirements come from?

Tell me about a time you stepped outside your scope or took initiative on something that wasn't your responsibility.

Negative feedback you've received and what you did about it.

Tell me about difficult feedback you gave to someone else.

Working with someone who wasn't finishing their tasks on time.

Tell me about a conflict and how you resolved it.

Tell me about someone you mentored and how you helped them grow.

Two-year plan for your current major project.

Most important qualifications you value when hiring for your team. What kind of candidate fills the biggest gap in your current domain?

Notes

L5+ loops weigh initiative answers heavily. Two recent rejection feedback notes — both from candidates who passed all technical rounds — cited initiative as the specific BQ gap. Prepare 2–3 concrete stories where you started something outside your formal scope and saw it through.

One format devotes the full hour to two projects chosen by the candidate, then explores their details without a rigid prompt sequence. Treat it as a resume deep dive: establish context and ownership quickly, then be ready to defend decisions, trade-offs, and outcomes from either project.

The end-to-end-ownership question is graded on whether you can describe both the upstream (where did the requirement come from, how was it scoped) and the downstream (how did you measure success, what was the outcome) — not just the implementation in the middle.

HM rounds drill on specifics when you name a concept. If you mention "online learning" or "calibration" or "backpressure" in passing, expect a follow-up that asks for a concrete example from your project. Don't drop terms you can't back up.

The 2-year-project-plan question is unusual for a BQ round but recurs in MLE loops. It is graded as a thinking-clarity signal as much as a planning signal; structure the answer as (current state → 6-month next steps → 18-month vision → key risks).

The "qualifications you value when hiring" question is the closest thing to a leveling probe — a junior-flavored answer ("strong coding skills") underperforms; a senior-flavored answer ("complementary skill profile to my current team's gap") lands.

Preparation

Pre-write 5 STAR stories covering: led-project-end-to-end, took-initiative, received-negative-feedback, worked-with-underperformer, cross-team-conflict. Rehearse each in 3 minutes.

For each story, prepare two depth layers: the surface narrative and a technical-detail follow-up the interviewer will ask. Don't tell stories you can't drill into.

Pre-draft the 2-year-project plan for your current main project — current state, 6-month next steps, 18-month vision, key risks — in writing.

Rehearse the hiring-bar answer with a senior framing. Identify the actual gap in your current team and have an honest answer for what you'd hire for.
