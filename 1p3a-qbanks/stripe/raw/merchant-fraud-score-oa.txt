Merchant Fraud Score (OA)
OA. Given transactions, fraud-scoring rules, and a baseline merchant score, compute each merchant's final fraud score by applying rule contributions over their transactions.

Requirements

Inputs:

transaction = ["merchant1,980,customer1,15", ...] — each row contains merchant_id, amount, customer ID, and a timestamp.

rules = ["1500,2,8,12", ...] — each rule contains a threshold and additive score values keyed to transaction properties.

merchants = ["merchant1,20", ...] — the base score per merchant.

Output: ["merchant1,50", ...] — the final score per merchant after applying every matching rule across that merchant's transactions.

Notes

The rule grammar is the most ambiguous part; exact semantics for every score component remain incomplete.

The implementation is roughly 50 lines in a common interview language.

This prompt family also appears in phone screens where AI assistance may be explicitly permitted. Confirm the live round's tool rules before starting.

Solution skeleton

Parse transactions, rules, and merchant baselines into typed records, then initialize one running score per merchant.

Keep the unrecovered grammar behind two small functions—one that decides whether a rule matches a transaction and one that returns its score contribution—so parsing, evaluation, and aggregation can be tested independently without assuming missing semantics.

For T transactions, R rules, and M merchants, the direct evaluator is O(T·R + M) time with O(M) score storage beyond the parsed input. Add an index only if the clarified rule grammar exposes a useful lookup key.

Preparation

Drill rule-engine problems: parse a list of rules, group transactions, and accumulate per-entity totals.

Pre-write a small apply_rule(transaction, rule, current_score) skeleton so multiple rules remain composable.

Practice CSV-style parsing and integer arithmetic; do not assume floating-point money values.
