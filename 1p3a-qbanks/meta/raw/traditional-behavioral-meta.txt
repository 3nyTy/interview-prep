Traditional Behavioral — Meta

Requirements

Reported question backbone (E5 and below):

Ambiguity — "How do you deal with ambiguity?" — pick a project where requirements/direction were unclear; show how you broke the problem down and drove forward.

Conflict — "How do you deal with conflicts?" — interviewer commonly pauses mid-answer and probes further; signal collaboration over avoidance or escalation.

Most-proud project — not just what you built, but why it mattered and your specific contribution.

Role model — usually with an intern/early-career framing: pick a colleague you admire and articulate the trait.

Self-improvement — clear-eyed answer about edges you're still growing; not faux-humility.

Additional common prompts: cross-team project, failed project, hard-to-collaborate teammate, giving constructive feedback, mentoring growth.

Cross-functional leadership — bring more than one example and be ready to switch stories when the interviewer asks for an alternative.

Incomplete information — describe a project you had to start before the facts or requirements were complete.

Direction change — explain how you adapted when a project changed course midway through execution.

Critical feedback — state the feedback precisely, the behavior you changed, and the evidence that the change stuck.

Competing stakeholder opinions — reconcile customers or stakeholders who advocate incompatible directions.

Execution pressure — cover a solution that failed midway, a suddenly accelerated deadline, a tight schedule, or work beyond your formal scope.

Influencing upward — persuade a manager to adopt your solution while making the trade-offs and dissent visible.

Learning and relationships — discuss a damaged teammate relationship and a concrete lesson learned from a senior engineer.

L6+ shift

L6 BQ is reportedly a different round even though questions look similar:

Scope & Impact — only cross-team / cross-org examples register. Single-team stories read as L5.

Influence without authority — how you got EM / PM / other Staff engineers aligned without reporting lines.

Decision-making under ambiguity — mature trade-off reasoning, not gut calls.

People & org impact — growing seniors into staff, changing team engineering bar / culture.

Failure stories at L6 = org-level failures: wrong architectural direction, underestimated cross-team cost. Emphasis on systemic fixes, not patches.

Notes

E5+ reports stress "leadership" weight in the BQ — bring at least 2-3 cross-team examples.

This round coexists with the new AI-Oriented BQ round (post-Apr-16). Candidates scheduled in the new loop may get either or both; do not drop the classic backbone.

Some BQ slots are run by EMs and turn into project deep-dives — expect 3-5 follow-ups on a single story.

Interviewers may reject the first example as insufficiently differentiated and ask for another. Prepare multiple cross-functional leadership stories with distinct scopes and outcomes.

Expect every story to be probed for your exact action, decision rationale, supporting data, measurable result, and what you would change on a second attempt.

Preparation

Build a story matrix: 6-8 stories × these axes (ambiguity, conflict, impact, failure, mentorship, push-back, leadership). Each story should hit ≥3 axes.

For L6+: re-cast every story toward org-level scope. "My team" → "three teams across two orgs"; "I shipped" → "I aligned the orgs to ship."

Pre-rehearse the silent-pause behavior: when the interviewer says "let me think about it," don't fill the silence — wait for the follow-up.

Read the L6 BQ debrief end-to-end if targeting Staff.
