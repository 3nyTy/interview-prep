News Feed / Timeline
Design a ranked news feed for a read-heavy social product with seconds-level freshness. Use a hybrid fan-out strategy, lightweight per-user inboxes, asynchronous celebrity delivery, bounded history, two-stage ranking, and explicit read-your-writes handling for a user's own posts.

Requirements

Functional:

Build each user's feed from content published by accounts they follow.

Rank results rather than returning a purely reverse-chronological list; the ranking model itself is out of scope.

Keep only a bounded set of recent inbox entries and retrieve older content through the read path.

Guarantee that a user's own newly published content appears immediately in that user's feed.

Scale and latency:

Reads greatly outnumber writes.

Seconds-level delay is acceptable for ordinary follower delivery.

High-fan-out creators may have millions of followers, so synchronously materializing every follower inbox on publish is not acceptable.

Design decisions:

Use fan-out on write for ordinary accounts and fan-out on read for high-fan-out creators. Merge both sources at read time.

Store content IDs and ranking scores in the inbox; fetch the content body separately to keep inbox storage light.

Use an asynchronous queue and batched work to smooth ordinary fan-out.

Compute a base ranking score on write, then combine it with real-time features in a lightweight read-time rerank.

Cache inboxes for active users; load inactive users on demand and apply LRU eviction.

Notes

State the push-versus-pull trade-off before drawing components. The hybrid boundary is the central decision, not an implementation afterthought.

Read-your-writes for a user's own post needs an explicit synchronous path; an asynchronous fan-out queue alone cannot provide it.

Keep the inbox representation separate from the content store so ranking and retention changes do not duplicate large content bodies.

Preparation

Quantify a celebrity fan-out and show where the design switches from write-time to read-time work.

Draw the publish, inbox-materialization, cache, and feed-read paths separately, then explain where they merge.

Rehearse the consistency discussion for ordinary follower delivery versus immediate visibility of the author's own post.
