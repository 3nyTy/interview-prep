Requirements

Use a portfolio slide deck to present previous QA work, then answer technical questions from the team. The hiring-manager screen also examines this experience.

Explain how you handled benchmark testing and regression testing, and what you optimized in each.

Explain how you reduced false failures when repeated regression runs could produce different valid results.

Describe how you reduced HPC resource consumption and benchmark runtime, including the improvement achieved.

Discuss whether test outcomes need finer classifications than pass, fail, and false failure: distinguish major crashes from less severe failures.

Explain who reviews benchmark performance, whether an evaluation scale exists, and how you decide that a benchmark result is acceptable.

Explain how deviations from a baseline become major issues or known issues, including the manager's role in that assessment.

Notes

Panel formats vary: one candidate described a half-hour presentation to eight people, while another described approximately one hour with six people. The recorded duration uses the upper end; presentation time and total discussion time may differ.

One candidate handled nondeterministic regression outcomes with a matrix matching all acceptable results. Their HPC optimization used different resource-usage levels during daytime and nighttime, and they described generating spreadsheet charts with scripts in the benchmark workflow. These are candidate project details, not required implementations.

The discussion distinguished classifying test cases by purpose from classifying the severity of their results. A candidate who had only used pass/fail found the finer-grained evaluation questions difficult.

The two Accessories accounts describe closely overlapping interview sequences and project histories; their independence is uncertain, so they do not establish that this is a newly introduced round.

For preparation, structure a QA project explanation as test objective -> acceptance criteria -> observed failure -> investigation -> change -> validation. Keep test purpose, correctness, and failure severity separate: an output can be incorrect without causing a major crash. For nondeterministic results, define acceptable outcomes or invariants before evaluating the run; do not widen acceptance merely to make a failing test pass.

Compare benchmark changes under a consistent workload and environment, using repeated runs to expose variability. Evaluate runtime and total resource consumption separately: a faster run can consume more resources. Explain the baseline, acceptance criteria, evidence for a regression, and who owns the decision to accept or investigate it. These are general preparation principles, not a prescribed Apple evaluation scale.

Preparation

In 15 minutes, outline a five-minute presentation of one QA project: test objective, baseline, your change, measured result, and one limitation. Rehearse a follow-up on your own contribution.

In 20 minutes, sketch an acceptable-results matrix for a regression test from your own work. Define how each result is checked, separate unexpected results from accepted alternatives, and assign failure severity independently of test purpose.

In 20 minutes, prepare a before/after benchmark comparison from your own work. List workload and environment controls, repeat-run variability, runtime, and resource consumption; explain who would review an unacceptable deviation without inventing an Apple-specific threshold.
