Onsite Behavioral: Proud Project / Conflict / Failure

The onsite behavioral round asks for a project the candidate is most proud of, a conflict or failure example, the biggest challenge faced, advice for the current team, and motivation for changing jobs. Prepare concise stories that connect ownership, technical judgment, and collaboration outcomes.

SWE

ownership
conflict
medium
Frequency
Low · New
Last asked
2026-08-01
Stage
onsite-bq
Requirements
Prepare a story for a project you are most proud of.
Prepare a conflict / disagreement story.
Prepare a failure story.
Prepare a story about the biggest challenge you have faced.
Be ready to explain what advice or change you would recommend to your current team and why.
Explain your motivation for changing jobs.
Each answer should include your role, the concrete technical or product stakes, what you personally did, and the outcome.
Notes
These prompts are usually paired with a mid-level onsite that also contains one coding round and one system-design round. The behavioral round is not a formality; it can carry final-loop signal.
Keep the proud-project story technical enough for follow-up questions. Snowflake interviewers can use it to probe scope, ownership, and how you reasoned through trade-offs.
Conflict and failure answers should end with a changed behavior, not just a resolved incident.
Preparation
Write one STAR-format proud-project story with metrics and one technical trade-off.
Prepare a conflict story where the disagreement was substantive, not interpersonal.
Prepare a failure story where you own the miss and can name the process or engineering change that followed.
Draft a specific team-improvement recommendation and connect it to evidence, trade-offs, and expected impact.
Practice a concise job-change explanation that stays forward-looking.
