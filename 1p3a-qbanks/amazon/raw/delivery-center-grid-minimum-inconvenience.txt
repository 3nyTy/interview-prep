Delivery-Center Grid — Minimum Inconvenience
Given an `n × m` binary grid of delivery centers, minimize the maximum Chebyshev distance from any empty cell to its nearest center after converting at most one `0` to `1`. Constraints are `n, m ≤ 500`.

Requirements

Input: an n × m binary grid, where 1 is a delivery center and 0 is an empty location.

Cell distance is Chebyshev distance: max(|r1-r2|, |c1-c2|).

Grid inconvenience is the maximum, over every empty cell, of its distance to the nearest delivery center.

Convert at most one 0 to 1 and return the minimum achievable inconvenience.

Constraints: 1 ≤ n, m ≤ 500; every cell is 0 or 1.

Examples

Input:
[[0, 0, 0, 1],
 [0, 0, 0, 1]]

Output: 1

Adding a center at (0, 0) leaves every cell within distance 1 of a center.

Notes

Compute nearest-center Chebyshev distance with multi-source BFS over all eight neighboring directions; a four-neighbor BFS would compute Manhattan distance instead. If there is no initial center, treat every empty cell's current distance as unbounded.

For a candidate limit L, collect empty cells whose current distance exceeds L. A new center (r, c) must lie in every square [ri-L, ri+L] × [ci-L, ci+L]; intersect those row and column intervals with the grid bounds. The limit is feasible exactly when both intersections are nonempty.

Binary-search the smallest feasible limit. The initial BFS is O(nm), each feasibility scan is O(nm), and the full search is O(nm log(max(n, m))).

The 500 × 500 limit makes repeated full searches per candidate placement too slow; the feasibility check must scan the grid compactly.

Preparation

Implement eight-neighbor multi-source grid BFS and verify its Chebyshev-distance matrix on grids with one center, many centers, and no initial center.

Practice binary search on an answer with a separate feasible(limit) predicate, then hand-check the interval intersections at corners and edges.
