BQ — Leadership Principles Standard Set
Every Amazon round runs 20-30 minutes of LP-anchored BQ. The recurring stems across 2026 loops are tight deadline, conflict, dive deep, out-of-scope contribution, tough feedback (given and received), disagree-and-commit, calculated risk, and customer obsession.

Requirements

Cover all 16 LPs with at least one STAR story each; have backup stories for the high-frequency LPs.

Be ready for follow-up depth: "what would you do differently," "dive deeper," "what was the trade-off," "who pushed back."

Tie every story to a quantified business outcome.

Additional story prompts: helping a struggling peer; your biggest mistake; proactively improving a company process; and applying something newly learned at work.

Applied Scientist breadth interviews may require ML content inside the behavioral story and then use it as the starting point for technical follow-ups.

Examples

Verbatim stems pulled from 2026 Q2 loops:

"Describe a time when you had a tight deadline but multiple workstreams."

"Conflict with a peer; how did you resolve it?"

"Out-of-responsibility work you took on."

"Tough feedback you received / gave."

"Tell me about a time you handled an ambiguous requirement."

"Disagree and commit — when did you push back, then deliver?"

"Risky decision you made under uncertainty."

"Quick decision under ambiguity."

"Dive deep on a project — bring numbers."

"Describe how you organized a project or product end-to-end."

"Tell me about a time you missed a deadline."

"A time you held your position when you believed everyone else was wrong."

"What technical optimization did you deliver for a customer?"

"Describe a technical problem you solved for a customer."

"Tell me about a time you turned a seemingly impossible project into something achievable."

"Describe a time you learned something new very quickly."

"Tell me about a time you refused a customer request."

"Tell me about a time you improved a product by better understanding user needs."

"Describe a time you delivered under a tight deadline, including the sacrifice, quality impact, and final outcome."

"Tell me about a technical approach you changed to improve the customer experience."

"Tell me about a judgment that data proved wrong. Which signal changed your mind, and how quickly did you act?"

"Describe a time you disagreed, committed after the decision, and then actively reduced the risk you had raised."

"What is a real development area for you, and what concrete change have you made?"

Manager-loop prompts:

"Tell me about a time you dealt with a low performer."

"How do you balance challenging work and team health?"

"How do you motivate people working on the team?"

Notes

HM / Bar Raiser rounds drill a single story 5+ layers deep. Pick stories where you genuinely owned the outcome and remember exact numbers.

Several interviewers reward proactive clarification ("I'm glad you ask that"); treat under-specified BQs the same way you treat under-specified coding prompts.

FAR / Premium-tier teams probe relentlessly on "what would you do differently" — every story needs a credible iteration beat.

Each interviewer is assigned 1-3 LPs in advance and scores you on whether you meet / raise / fall below the bar for those specific principles — the same story can be reused across rounds because the lens differs.

Customer Obsession is the de-facto north-star LP; weave a customer outcome into every story even when the prompt targets a different LP.

Keep roughly 80% of the narrative in first-person 'I' actions. Stories that drift into 'we' / 'the team' lose the individual-contribution signal Bar Raisers look for.

The Bar Raiser and the hiring manager are the only interviewers with veto authority — they own the LP and behavioral signal more than the technical signal.

For MLE loops, the project deep-dive and LP evaluation may explicitly judge whether prior work had enough impact and scale. Prepare the numbers behind scope, users, revenue, latency, or model-quality movement, not just the technical narrative.

Current VO loops keep LP in every technical slot, including coding and system design. Prepare for a short LP opener or closer even when the calendar invite names the round as purely technical.

In-person NG loops now run four back-to-back rounds in roughly four hours and can pack 15+ LP prompts across them — most rounds interleave ~30 minutes technical with ~30 minutes BQ, and the final round is pure BQ. A single loop can sweep ten or more LPs (Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit, Invent and Simplify, Insist on the Highest Standards, Are Right A Lot, Deliver Results, Dive Deep, Customer Obsession, Ownership, Learn and Be Curious, Earn Trust), so story-bank breadth matters as much as depth — and plan physically: candidates describe losing their voice by round four.

Preparation

Build a 16x2 STAR matrix: 16 LPs × 2 stories each, all with metric outcomes and a 'do differently' answer.

Drill the 'dive deep' follow-up by recording yourself answering the same BQ for 5 minutes, then again for 10.

Pre-rehearse 'why Amazon' and 'why this team' — these still show up surprisingly often.

Source candidate stories by listing the 3-5 most impactful projects from the last few years, then map each to 2-3 LPs it can serve — most candidates over-prepare unique stories per LP when one strong story flexes across several.

Drill the metric layer: for every story, memorize the exact baseline number, the delta, and the business impact. "Data-driven" is the explicit rubric word in Amazon's own prep guidance.

Practice the 'circular narrative': open with the problem, spend the body on personal actions with metrics, close by returning to how the problem was resolved. This holds up better under 10-15 minutes of dive-deep probing than a linear STAR recitation.
